Question 4

For price and carat, report the mean, median, minimum and maximum. Is price right-skewed? Justify using the mean and the median.

df[['price', 'carat']].agg(['mean', 'median', 'min', 'max']).round(2)
Answer. Price: mean 3,932.80, median 2,401, minimum 326, maximum 18,823. Carat: mean 0.80, median 0.70, minimum 0.20, maximum 5.01. Price is right-skewed: its mean is well above its median (skewness 1.62), because a few very expensive diamonds pull the mean up.
4 marks

Question 5

How many diamonds are there of each cut? Which cut is the most common, and what percentage of all diamonds is it?

counts = df['cut'].value_counts()
print(counts)
print('Share of the most common cut:', round(100 * counts.iloc[0] / len(df), 1), '%')
Answer. Ideal 21,551; Premium 13,791; Very Good 12,082; Good 4,906; Fair 1,610. Ideal is the most common, at 40.0% of all diamonds.
6 marks

Question 6

Compute the mean price for each cut, sorted from highest to lowest. The best cut (Ideal) has the lowest mean price. Compute the mean carat for each cut and use it to explain why.

print(df.groupby('cut', observed=True)['price'].mean().sort_values(ascending=False).round(0))
print(df.groupby('cut', observed=True)['carat'].mean().round(2))
Answer. Mean price: Premium 4,584; Fair 4,359; Very Good 3,982; Good 3,929; Ideal 3,458. Mean carat: Fair 1.05; Premium 0.89; Good 0.85; Very Good 0.81; Ideal 0.70. Ideal diamonds are the smallest on average, and size (carat) drives price far more than cut, so comparing cuts without allowing for carat is misleading (a confounding variable).
4 marks

Question 7

Plot a histogram of price. Describe its shape in one sentence.

sns.histplot(data=df, x='price', bins=50)
plt.title('Distribution of diamond prices')
plt.show()
Answer. Strongly right-skewed: most diamonds cost under about 5,000 (75% cost 5,324 or less), with a long tail up to 18,823.
4 marks

Question 8

Draw a scatter plot of carat (x) against price (y). Use transparency or a random sample of 5,000 rows so the points are readable. Describe the relationship.

sample = df.sample(5000, random_state=1)
sns.scatterplot(data=sample, x='carat', y='price', alpha=0.3)
plt.title('Price against carat (5,000 diamonds)')
plt.show()
Answer. A strong, positive, non-linear relationship: price rises faster than carat, and the spread of prices widens for larger stones. Points cluster at popular sizes such as 0.3, 0.7 and 1.0 carat.
4 marks

Question 9

Draw a box plot of price for each color. Which colour has the highest median price? (D is the best colour and J the worst.)

sns.boxplot(data=df, x='color', y='price')
plt.title('Price by colour')
plt.show()
print(df.groupby('color', observed=True)['price'].median().sort_values(ascending=False))
Answer. J has the highest median price (4,234), then I (3,730) and H (3,460); D (1,838) and E (1,739) are the lowest. Again a size effect: J stones tend to be larger.
6 marks

Question 10

Compute the correlation matrix of the numeric columns. Which variable is most strongly correlated with price, and what is the correlation? (Optional: show it as a heatmap.)

corr = df.select_dtypes('number').corr()
print(corr['price'].sort_values(ascending=False).round(3))
sns.heatmap(corr, annot=True, fmt='.2f', cmap='Blues')
plt.show()
Answer. Carat is the most strongly correlated with price (r = 0.922), followed by the dimensions x (0.884), y (0.865) and z (0.861). Depth (−0.011) and table (0.127) are barely related to price.
