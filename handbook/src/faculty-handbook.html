Exam Proctor: Faculty Handbook

How to run a class test with Exam Proctor · Version 1 · October 2026

Exam Proctor lets you decide which websites students may use during an online test, and shows you live, on one screen, any student who goes elsewhere. It uses no camera, microphone or screen recording. This handbook takes you from your first sign-in to reviewing the results.

1. What it does, and what it doesn't

It does

  • Check each student's active tab against your list of allowed sites
  • Warn the student on screen the moment they leave it
  • Show you every student live, with problems first, and raise a flag within seconds
  • Notice switching to another app, other open tabs, a closed browser, a removed extension
  • Keep a timeline of every page visited, for review after the test

It doesn't

  • See phones, second computers, books or people in the room
  • See other browsers (only that the student left Chrome)
  • Record keystrokes, page content, the screen, camera or microphone
  • Block anything: it warns and records
  • Decide misconduct: every flag is a prompt for your judgement

Students install a free Chrome extension once, then join each test with a test code (which you give them in advance), their roll number, and a PIN that you announce at the start. Monitoring runs only between the test's start and end times.

2. Before your first test

  1. Get an account. Email nikhildj@xime.org with your name, preferred username and how many tests you plan to run this term. You will receive an invigilator account with a temporary password and an exam limit.
  2. Sign in at the dashboard address above. The first visit of the day can take up to a minute while the server wakes up.
  3. Change your password straight away: My account → Change my password (at least 10 characters).
  4. Tell Nikhil about your test (date, time and class size) when you schedule it, so he can help on the day and plan around other large tests.

Your account

  • You see and manage only the tests you create. Colleagues cannot see yours, and you cannot see theirs. Admins can see every test.
  • Your exam limit is the most tests you can have at once (shown on the Exams page, for example "2 of 5 used"). Delete old tests, after exporting their flags, or ask Nikhil to raise it.
  • Test codes are shared across the institute: name tests COURSE-SECTION-TEST, for example BA101-A-QUIZ2. If a code is taken, choose another.
  • A colleague helping you invigilate cannot open your test from their own account. They watch on your signed-in screen, or ask an admin.

3. Planning a test

Is Exam Proctor right for this test?

It works best for timed online tests where students should use only a few specific resources: an online quiz (Google Forms, Microsoft Forms, the LMS), a notebook platform, a calculator or documentation. For a test taken entirely on paper, or one that needs many websites, it adds little.

Choosing the allowed sites

List every site a student genuinely needs, including the sign-in page your quiz platform sends them through. Anything not listed is flagged. Each line can be:

You writeWhat it allows
kaggle.comThe whole site and its subdomains (www.kaggle.com), every page
docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQ…Only that one Google Form. Google Docs, Sheets and other forms stay flagged
desmos.com/calculatorOnly the Desmos calculator, not the rest of Desmos

Google Forms: remove /viewform from the end

When you paste a form link, delete /viewform (and anything after it). After a student presses Submit, Google moves them to …/formResponse; with /viewform on your line, that page would be flagged for the whole class. Appendix A lists tested entries for common platforms.

The other settings

SettingWhat to choose

4. Creating the test in the dashboard

  1. Click + New exam and fill in the name, code, start, end, PIN and grace period.
  2. Type or paste the Allowed sites, one per line. Read the preview under the box: each line says whether it allows a whole site or only part of one.
  3. Paste every link your test uses into Test a link. Each must say Allowed. Also try one that should be flagged.
  4. Paste the Class roster: one student per line, roll number then name. You can copy two columns straight from Excel.
  5. Click Create exam. The test appears in the list as scheduled.

Run a practice test first

Create a 10-minute test called, say, BA101-A-PRACTICE a few days before the real one, with the same allowed sites. Students install the extension and join it once. Installation problems then surface before they matter, and you can check your allowed-sites list with real students.

5. Preparing your students

  1. One week before: email the class the Student Guide PDF, the test code and the date (template in Appendix C). Say clearly what is and isn't recorded.
  2. Offer an alternative to any student who doesn't consent, can't use Chrome, or has accessibility needs. Ask them to tell you before the test.
  3. Two or three days before: run the practice test, or at least ask everyone to install and pin the extension.
  4. The day before: send a reminder (Appendix C): Chrome, the extension, signed in to any accounts the test needs (for example Google), laptop charged.

7. After the test: reviewing flags

  1. Wait 15 minutes after the end, then open the test's live view.
  2. For each student with flags, open their panel and read the flag together with the timeline and activity log. Context matters more than a single flag.
  3. Dismiss flags with an innocent explanation and Confirm those that need follow-up. Add a short note either way; it records your name.
  4. Click Export flags (CSV) and keep the file with your test records.
  5. Follow the institute's academic integrity process for anything confirmed. A flag is never enough on its own: show the student the record and hear their explanation.
FlagCommon innocent causesWhat to check

Records are deleted 90 days after each test, unless a case is under review. Ask Nikhil to keep a test's records longer if a case is open.

8. Limits and responsible use

  • Design questions that reward understanding. Monitoring deters casual misuse; it cannot see a phone or a friend in the room.
  • Tell students exactly what is recorded, and never present the tool as more powerful than it is.
  • Keep the allowed list tight but complete. Each extra site is extra freedom; each missing one is a false alarm for the whole class.
  • Platforms that host other people's work (Kaggle, GitHub) are allowed as a whole site. Students could browse others' public notebooks there without a flag; check the activity logs.
  • Students can switch the extension off on their own laptops. That is flagged, not prevented. On institution-managed laptops, IT can force-install it.
  • Handle the data with care: it identifies students. Share CSVs only with people who need them.

9. Troubleshooting

ProblemFix
The dashboard is slow to openThe server sleeps when unused and wakes in about a minute. Always open it 15 minutes before a test.
"Wrong username or password"Usernames are case-sensitive. After 8 wrong tries, sign-in from that network locks for 10 minutes; ask Nikhil to reset your password.
"You have reached your limit of N exams"Export the flags of an old test and delete it, or ask Nikhil to raise your limit.
"An exam with this code already exists"Codes are unique across the institute. Add your section or the term, for example BA101-A-QUIZ2-OCT.
Student: "No exam with this code"Check the spelling; codes are not case-sensitive but must match exactly otherwise.
Student: "Not on the list for this exam"Their roll number is missing or mistyped in your roster. Edit the exam and fix it.
Student: "Incorrect exam PIN"PINs are case-sensitive. Repeat it slowly, or write it on the board.
Student: "Cannot reach the exam server"Their internet, or the server waking up. Wait a minute and retry.
Everyone flagged on the same pageA needed site is missing: use Allow <site> (section 6) and remember it for next time.
A student's laptop blocks extensionsManaged by another organisation's IT. Offer the alternative arrangement.
